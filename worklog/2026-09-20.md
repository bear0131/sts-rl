## TRAIN05 occupancy-distributed restart

User intention: "按照这个东西预热启动训练" and "尽快启动训练，效率高一些". Scope is a
new four-rank fork from the pinned TRAIN01 step10027008 bundles. Preserve model, Adam and replay;
replace synchronized fresh-environment startup with independent live-state occupancy sampling;
discard every warmup sample and begin the unchanged40-update production loop only after all
environments have accepted a real in-game state. The old stopped runs and source snapshots must
remain unchanged.

For a frozen policy/search trajectory `tau=(X_0,...,X_{L-1})`, `L<=H=6000`, each attempt draws
`A` uniformly from `0,...,H-1` and accepts iff `A<L`. Therefore
`P(tau,A=t | A<L)=P(tau)/E[L]` for `t<L`, and the accepted state distribution is
`E[sum_t 1{X_t in B}]/E[L]`, the renewal-reward random-decision-time occupancy measure. This is
not exact recovery of the missing old environment frontier and assumes a frozen transition kernel
during warmup. The finite fail-closed budget is131072 real decisions per unaccepted environment.
For any sequence of rejected episode lengths, the probability of remaining unaccepted after B
real decisions is at most `exp(-B/H)`; a union bound over384 environments is below`1.3e-7`.
Conditioning on finishing within a finite budget is therefore not literally zero bias, even though
the bound is small.

`StartupOccupancyWrapper` implements the rejection sampler and pauses accepted environments while
the remaining environments advance. Paused environments are excluded from upper rules, combat
search and combat sample construction. The startup boundary restores the formal global step,
leaves replay cursor/size/total-added unchanged, performs no optimizer update, resets the rollout
buffer and clears completed/pending combat roots, reasoning samples, episode buffers and logger
accumulators. `VecMonitor` episode length and return are restored to the accepted partial episode,
so the first formal completion has correct monitoring state. The production override retains rule
upper,20M target,40 NOSL updates, strict Full-Gumbel128, replay and all original batch settings; its
only startup change is `--startup-occupancy-budget 131072`.

### TRAIN05 startup win-rate investigation

User intention: investigate the anomalously high win rate, but make no correction if the issue is
only metric calculation; do not interrupt or mutate the live training. Read-only inspection found
all four trainer PIDs still alive and synchronized at step10321920. The six post-warmup dashboard
points were 18.42%, 12.92%, 12.22%, 11.99%, 10.49%, and 8.75% win rate at steps10076160 through
10321920, respectively; the next point at10371072 was10.50%. Over the first six points floor mean remained32.53-33.60 and Act1/Act2 clear
rates remained82.23-85.75% / 27.80-30.78%. The monotonic decay into the pre-restart9-10% range is
evidence against a persistent outcome or simulator bug.

The startup-only spike comes from an empty per-rank100-completed-episode rolling window observing
the finite incumbent cohort selected by occupancy warmup. That cohort is selected approximately
per transition (an episode enters with weight `min(L,1000)`), whereas `game/win_rate` counts each
completed episode once. Ordinary continuous training has already replaced that cohort through
natural resets and its full rolling window therefore observes recently completed, ordinarily
started episodes. This is why the startup boundary exposes a transient that normal training does
not. A direct cold-restart control in the source run showed the opposite startup transient: its
first five resumed points had0% win rate while floor mean climbed0,8.88,16.76,22.26,26.54; only the
sixth point reached2.91% / floor28.68. Thus an ordinary environment restart is also biased at the
chart boundary, but toward short early failures rather than toward the incumbent cohort. Episode
length alone is not a sufficient explanation for the exact first-window value; the
1000-step truncation and which incumbent residual lifetimes finish inside each rollout also matter.
The formal training samples and this completion-count chart use different measures, so this
transient does not by itself show a replay-distribution win bias. The warmup remains only the
documented first1000-decision occupancy approximation, not an exact infinite-horizon stationary
sample.

Static inspection also found that `game/win_rate` treats either `act > 3` or
`outcome == PLAYER_VICTORY` as a win, while `game/act_3_won_mean` tests only `act > 3`; this explains
why the latter can remain zero while win rate is positive and is an independent chart-definition
difference, not evidence that those wins were inserted into replay incorrectly. Distributed game
metrics are unweighted means of the four rank-local rolling means because `game/episodes_total` is
not part of the collective reduction, which can add small startup error when ranks have unequal
numbers of completions. Per user instruction, no metric, environment, replay, configuration,
process, or checkpoint was changed. Verification was the launcher read-only status plus direct
parsing of rank0 JSON metric records; this section accompanies the current documentation commit.
